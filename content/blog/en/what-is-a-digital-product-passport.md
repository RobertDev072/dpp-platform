A **digital product passport (DPP)** is an online record that belongs to a single product, batch or model. You open it by scanning a **QR code** or another data carrier on the product or its packaging. No app is needed: the passport opens in your phone's browser.

## Why is the EU introducing it?

The European Union wants products to last longer, be easier to repair and easier to recycle. That requires information that is often missing or scattered today: which materials are inside, where do they come from, how do you repair it, and what happens at end of life?

The foundation is the **Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR, Regulation (EU) 2024/1781)**. It entered into force in July 2024 and allows the EU to set requirements per product group, including a digital product passport.

## What does a passport contain?

It depends on the product group and is set per group in a delegated act. Common elements are:

- **identification**: product name, model, manufacturer, unique identifier;
- **materials and composition**, including substances of concern;
- **sustainability**: carbon footprint, recycled content, expected lifetime;
- **use and repair**: manuals, spare parts, repair instructions;
- **end of life**: how to return, dismantle or recycle the product;
- **documents**, such as the EU declaration of conformity.

Not everything is visible to everyone. Consumers see the public information; some data is meant only for repairers, recyclers or market surveillance authorities.

## Which products will need one?

The rollout is gradual:

| Product group | Status (October 2026) |
|---|---|
| Electric vehicle, light means of transport (e-bike) and industrial batteries above 2 kWh | **Mandatory from 18 February 2027** (Batteries Regulation) |
| Iron and steel, aluminium | Priority in the ESPR working plan; rules in preparation |
| Textiles (mainly apparel), tyres | Priority; delegated act expected around 2027 |
| Furniture, mattresses | Priority; later in the working plan |
| Construction products | Phased in under the new Construction Products Regulation |

Note: under the ESPR there are, as a rule, **at least 18 months** between the adoption of a delegated act and the date its requirements apply. You have time, but collecting supplier data often takes months too.

## Who has to create the passport?

The **manufacturer** or **importer** placing the product on the EU market is responsible. Retailers and web shops must make sure customers can access the passport. You can outsource the work to a service provider, but the responsibility stays with you.

## What about the EU registry?

Since July 2026 the **EU DPP registry** has been live. It does not hold the full passport, but the product's unique identifier plus some registration data. The passport itself stays with the company or its service provider: the EU deliberately chose a decentralised system.
