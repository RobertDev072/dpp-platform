The **battery passport** is the first digital product passport to become truly mandatory in the EU. The date is fixed: **18 February 2027**. From then on, every battery in the covered categories placed on the market needs its own electronic passport, accessible through a QR code.

## Which batteries?

Under Article 77 of the **Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542**, the passport applies to:

- **electric vehicle batteries**;
- **light means of transport (LMT) batteries**, such as e-bikes, e-scooters and electric mopeds;
- **industrial batteries with a capacity above 2 kWh**, for example home and business storage batteries for solar power.

Portable batteries (such as in a phone or remote control) and starter batteries do not need a battery passport. Other rules of the regulation, such as labelling, do apply to them.

## Per battery, not per model

An important difference from many other product groups: the battery passport belongs to **each individual battery**. Two batteries of the same model each have their own passport with their own unique identifier.

## What does it contain?

The regulation lists, among other things:

- identification of the battery and manufacturer;
- chemistry and **material composition**, including critical raw materials;
- **carbon footprint**;
- **recycled content**;
- **performance and durability**, such as capacity and expected lifetime;
- information on dismantling, reuse and recycling.

Part of it is public; part is only accessible to, for example, repairers, recyclers and authorities.

## Who is responsible?

The company that **places the battery on the EU market** (manufacturer or importer). You can use a service provider to create and manage the passports, but you remain responsible.

## What you can do now

1. **Determine which batteries are in scope.** Take the [DPP check](/en/dpp-check).
2. **Request data from your suppliers**: cell maker, materials, carbon footprint. This usually takes longest.
3. **Arrange unique identifiers per battery** and a way to put the QR code on the battery.
4. **Choose a platform** that handles passports per item, keeps versions and provides a QR code that keeps working.
5. **Test with a few batteries** well before February 2027.

> Just over three months to go. Starting now leaves time to fix mistakes before the deadline.
