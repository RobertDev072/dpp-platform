**Textiles, and apparel in particular,** sit at the top of the European Commission's priority list. In the **ESPR working plan 2025–2030** (adopted in April 2025) it is one of the first product groups to receive ecodesign requirements and a digital product passport.

## When will it become mandatory?

As of October 2026, **no delegated act for textiles has been adopted yet**. The expectation is:

- **2027**: adoption of the delegated act for textiles;
- then, as a rule, a **transition period of at least 18 months**;
- so the passport is expected to become mandatory **at the earliest in late 2028 or 2029**.

Exact dates are only fixed once the act is published in the Official Journal. We will update this article when that happens.

## What will it likely contain?

The exact list follows from the delegated act, but likely items are:

- fibre composition and **recycled content**;
- **origin** and production sites;
- **durability and lifetime** information;
- **care and repair** instructions;
- information on **substances of concern**;
- how to return or recycle the product.

## Already in force: destruction ban

Separate from the passport, since 19 July 2026 **large companies** may no longer destroy unsold apparel and footwear. Medium-sized companies follow later.

## What you can do now

1. **Collect your data per product**: composition, suppliers, countries of production.
2. **Agree with your suppliers** which information they must provide.
3. **Start a voluntary passport** for one collection. Customers value transparency, and you learn how the process works.
4. **Take the [DPP check](/en/dpp-check)** to review your situation.
