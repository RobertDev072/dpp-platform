In July 2026 the European Commission launched the **digital product passport registry**, together with a testing environment. The rules are set out in **Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778**, adopted on 16 July 2026, which builds on the Ecodesign Regulation (ESPR).

## What is the registry?

The registry is an **index**, not a database of all product information. Companies register:

- the product's **unique identifier** (per model, batch or item, depending on the rules for that product group);
- a limited set of **mandatory registration data**.

After successful validation each registration receives its own registration identifier.

## What is *not* in it?

The **passport itself**. The content (materials, documents, carbon footprint, repair information) stays with the company or its service provider. The EU deliberately chose a **decentralised system**: no central database, but one place where authorities and customs can check that a passport exists.

## How do you register?

Through a **web interface** or an **API**. For companies with many products the API is the logical route; a DPP platform can automate it for you. The Commission also offers guidance, webinars and a helpdesk.

## What does it mean for you?

- **Batteries**: the first real deadline is **18 February 2027**. From then on, passports for the covered batteries must exist and be registered.
- **Other product groups**: registration becomes mandatory once the delegated act for your product group applies.
- **Practise now**: the testing environment is there to try the connection in advance.

## Passport and registration: two separate steps

Many companies mix them up. In short: the **passport** is what the customer sees when scanning the QR code; the **registration** is the entry in the EU registry stating that the passport exists. You need both.
